# LinkedIn Job Scam Guide

> Especially relevant for crypto/Web3 job seekers.

## Red Flags

- Salary range offered upfront, before any interview
- Agency + client you can't find online (no LinkedIn, no Glassdoor)
- "Token bonuses" with no details — what token, what's it worth, is it liquid
- Client website doesn't load or is behind a captcha
- They ask you to install something ("test task", "our work tool", "our messenger")
- They ask for passport data, home address, or document photos
- Urgency pressure: "we need someone ASAP", "offer expires today"
- Recruiter contacts you from personal Gmail or Telegram, not corporate email
- Job description is vague but sounds too good to be true

## Signs of a Legitimate Offer

- Company LinkedIn page with real history and real employees
- Glassdoor / Clutch reviews
- Multiple interview rounds before an offer
- They can name specific team members with verifiable profiles
- Payment happens after work is delivered, not before
- Corporate email, working website with no suspicious captcha

## How to Verify

1. Ask for the company LinkedIn page — if they dodge the question, it's a scam
2. Google `"company name" scam` and `"company name" review`
3. Check the domain on whois — a young domain (.co, .xyz, registered recently) is a warning sign
4. Look up the recruiter on LinkedIn — do they have a real history and real connections?
5. Never install anything before signing an offer

## Known Schemes

**Lazarus Group (North Korea):**
- Approach engineers on LinkedIn with job offers, send a "test task"
- The task contains malware that drains crypto wallets
- Stole $308M in a single attack (FBI report, 2025)
- Pattern: fake recruiter + real-sounding client name to build trust

**Fake agency + fake client:**
- A non-existent agency acts as recruiter, uses a real company's name as the client
- Goal: collect your CV with personal data, or get malware installed via a "test task"

## The One Rule

Never install anything before you have a signed offer. That's the main attack vector.
