# LinkedIn Summary/About Section: Best Practices 2025-2026

> Research for senior backend engineers (Go/Rust/AI). March 2026.

---

## 1. Optimal Structure — The 4-Part Formula

Consensus across multiple sources points to a **Hook → Bridge → Proof → CTA** structure:

| Section | Lines | Purpose |
|---------|-------|---------|
| **Hook** | 1-3 | Bold opening. Stops the scroll. Must work above the fold |
| **Bridge** | 4-8 | Context, audience, credibility. Who you help, what problem you solve |
| **Proof** | 9-14 | 3-6 scannable bullet points with measurable results |
| **CTA** | 15-17 | Clear next step: DM, email, link to portfolio |

**Paragraphs:** 4-6 short paragraphs total. Mobile optimization demands max 2-3 lines per paragraph. Use line breaks generously — walls of text kill engagement.

**Alternative framework (Problem-Agitate-Solution):**
1. Who do you help? (define your audience)
2. What problem do you solve? (pain point)
3. Why are you the right person? (unique value + proof)
4. What should they do next? (CTA)

## 2. First 2-3 Lines — Above the "See More" Fold

**This is the single most critical element.**

- **Desktop:** ~300 characters visible before "See more"
- **Mobile:** ~200 characters visible
- **Rule:** Your first 2-3 lines ARE your About section for 80%+ of visitors who never click "See more"

### What works above the fold:

**Pattern 1 — Identity + Impact:**
> "Senior Go engineer building distributed systems that handle $2B+ daily trading volume. 10+ years shipping backend infrastructure in crypto, fintech, and high-load consumer products."

**Pattern 2 — Hook question:**
> "What happens when your exchange backend needs to process $2B in daily volume with zero downtime? That's the kind of problem I solve."

**Pattern 3 — Currently doing + trajectory:**
> "Currently building open-source Rust market data tooling (LOB + Parquet). Previously: backend at CompanyB (100M+ MAU), CompanyC ($2B/day), CompanyD."

### What does NOT work above the fold:
- "I am a passionate engineer who..."
- "With over 10 years of experience..."
- Generic adjectives: innovative, motivated, detail-oriented
- Your life story starting from university

### For your profile specifically:
The current fake_profile summary starts with "Senior Go and Rust backend engineer with 10+ years..." — this is functional but generic. The "10+ years" opener is the LinkedIn equivalent of "Dear Sir/Madam." A stronger hook would lead with the most impressive metric or current activity.

## 4. Keywords Placement — Where They Matter Most

LinkedIn's search algorithm weights keywords differently by location:

| Priority | Location | Weight |
|----------|----------|--------|
| 1 | **Headline** | Highest — appears in search results, triggers recruiter clicks |
| 2 | **First 2-3 lines of About** | High — indexed prominently |
| 3 | **Job titles in Experience** | High — exact match for recruiter filters |
| 4 | **Skills section** | High — up to 50 skills, top 3 get most visibility |
| 5 | **Rest of About section** | Medium — natural repetition 2-3x total |
| 6 | **Experience descriptions** | Medium |

### Keyword strategy for Go/Rust/AI backend engineer:

**Primary keywords** (must appear in headline + first lines of About):
- Go / Golang
- Distributed Systems
- Backend Engineer / Senior Backend Engineer
- AI Agents (if targeting AI-native roles)

**Secondary keywords** (sprinkle through About + Experience):
- Rust, microservices, Kafka, PostgreSQL, Kubernetes
- Blockchain, crypto, fintech
- gRPC, event-driven architecture
- Claude Code, AI tooling, context engineering

**Anti-pattern:** Keyword stuffing. "Go Golang Go developer Go engineer Go programming" reads like spam. Natural repetition 2-3x is the sweet spot.

**Pro tip:** Read 10 job descriptions for your target role. Extract the exact phrases they use. Mirror those phrases in your About — recruiters search using the same vocabulary as job postings.

## 5. First Person vs Third Person

**Consensus: First person. Always.**

- First person sounds authentic, warm, approachable
- Third person sounds like a press release or like someone else wrote it
- LinkedIn itself recommends first person
- The only edge case for third person: C-suite executives reusing corporate bios (not relevant here)

For engineers specifically, first person creates a "talking to a colleague" feel that resonates with hiring managers and CTOs who scan profiles.

## 6. Call to Action — Yes, Include One

**Always end with a CTA.** Without it, a reader who likes your profile has no clear next step.

### CTA patterns that work for engineers seeking roles:

**Direct (active job search):**
> "Open to senior backend roles (Go/Rust) at AI-native or crypto/fintech companies. Remote preferred. DM me or reach out at your.email@example.com"

**Portfolio-driven:**
> "Check out my open-source work: github.com/YourName — or DM me to talk distributed systems."

**Conversation-starter:**
> "Always happy to talk about distributed systems, market microstructure, or AI-augmented development. Let's connect."

**Segmented (multiple audiences):**
> "Hiring? Let's talk — your.email@example.com
> Building something in crypto/AI? I might be able to help."

### CTA anti-patterns:
- No CTA at all (most common mistake)
- "Feel free to reach out" — too vague, passive
- CTA buried in the middle of the text — always put it last

## 7. "Currently" Section — Very Important

**Showing what you're doing NOW is one of the strongest signals,** especially when between jobs. It answers the recruiter's first question: "What is this person doing right now?"

### Why it matters:
- Eliminates the "gap" stigma
- Shows initiative and continuous growth
- Gives something concrete to talk about in interviews
- Open source work = verifiable proof of skills

### Patterns that work:

> "Currently: Building your-project — an open-source Rust market data collector (LOB + Parquet) for quantitative trading."

> "Right now I'm deep in Rust async, building real-time market data infrastructure for crypto trading."

> "Between roles, I'm shipping open-source tooling and exploring AI agent orchestration patterns."

### For your profile:
You have your-project (Rust market data collector) + AI agent orchestration (your-tool) — both are excellent "currently" signals. Your-project especially: it's Rust + open source + financial data + a gap in the market. This should be prominently featured.

## 8. Career Trajectory — Language Transitions as Strength

Framing Java → Go → Rust as a deliberate evolution is a **significant differentiator.** Most engineers get stuck in one ecosystem. Showing intentional migration signals:

- Intellectual curiosity
- Ability to learn fast
- Understanding of trade-offs between languages
- Adaptability (critical for AI-era engineering)

### How to frame it:

**Bad:** "I know Java, Go, and Rust" (just a list)

**Good — Evolution narrative:**
> "Started in Java/JVM building enterprise systems, moved to Go for cloud-native microservices at scale (CompanyB, 100M+ MAU), now working in Rust for performance-critical market data infrastructure."

**Better — Each transition = a reason:**
> "Java → Go: needed the concurrency model and deployment simplicity for 1000+ RPS microservices. Go → Rust: needed zero-cost abstractions and memory safety guarantees for sub-millisecond market data processing."

**Best — Tie to outcomes:**
> "My path from Java (enterprise) → Go (cloud-native, CompanyB/CompanyA) → Rust (systems/performance) mirrors the industry's own evolution. Each transition was driven by the demands of the next problem: from enterprise stability → cloud-native speed → systems-level precision."

### Key framing principles:
- Each transition was **intentional**, not random
- Each move was driven by **technical need**, not trend-chasing
- The trajectory shows you pick **the right tool for the problem**
- You carry **architectural wisdom** across languages, not just syntax

## 9. AI-Assisted Development — How to Position

### The landscape (2025-2026):
- 73% of engineering teams use AI coding tools daily (2026 survey)
- "AI collaboration" appears in 42% of tech job postings
- AI engineering is the fastest-growing skill category on LinkedIn
- Developers fluent in AI workflows earn 20-40% more than peers who resist

### How top engineers position AI skills:

**DO — Frame as engineering discipline:**
> "Building AI-augmented development workflows: Claude Code skills for database queries, CI/CD automation, and observability. Shipped AI code-review bot that reduced review overhead by ~40%."

**DO — Show outcomes, not tools:**
> "Introduced AI-assisted development practices across a 379-microservice platform, measurably accelerating feature delivery and code review cycles."

**DO — Use the term "context engineering":**
> "Context engineering" (managing what AI agents know) is the 2026 term replacing "prompt engineering." It signals sophistication.

**DON'T — Just list tools:**
> "Skills: Claude Code, Cursor, GitHub Copilot" — this is the 2026 equivalent of listing "Microsoft Word" in 2010

**DON'T — Say "I use AI to write code for me":**
> This signals laziness. Frame it as orchestration, not delegation.

**DON'T — Overweight AI vs core engineering:**
> AI skills are a multiplier on strong fundamentals, not a replacement. If your About section is 80% about AI tools and 20% about engineering — you'll look like a hype-surfer.

### Sweet spot:
You have genuine AI engineering experience (not just using Copilot): Claude Code skills, AI review bot, multi-agent orchestration (your-tool), MCP protocol. This is **rare for a Go backend engineer** and is a genuine differentiator. Dedicate 1-2 sentences or a bullet to it, framed as engineering practice, not buzzword.
