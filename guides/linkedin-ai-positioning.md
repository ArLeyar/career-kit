## 2. The AI Role Taxonomy (key distinctions)

| Role | Focus | Builds | Trains Models? |
|------|-------|--------|----------------|
| **ML Engineer** | Model-focused | Training pipelines, feature engineering | Yes |
| **AI Engineer** | System-focused | Applications using pre-trained models (LLMs, APIs) | No (fine-tuning at most) |
| **AI Agent Developer** | Agent-focused | Autonomous agents, multi-agent workflows | No |
| **AI Infrastructure Engineer** | Infra-focused | Deployment, scaling, GPU clusters, MLOps | No |
| **AI Architect** | Design-focused | System architecture for AI products | No |

**Key insight:** "All ML engineers work in AI, but not all AI engineers work in ML." The AI Engineer role in 2026 is closer to product/backend engineering than to data science. It's about **integrating LLMs into production systems**, not training models.

**Salary range (2026):** AI Engineers focusing on agents/LLMs: $150k-$250k base. Specialized agentic AI: $175k-$250k.

| **AI-native** | "AI-native architecture/company" — signals modern thinking |

### Rising Fast (use to stand out)

|------|-------|

| **AI enthusiast** | Signals hobbyist, not professional |
| **Generative AI** | Too broad, everyone says it |
| **Machine Learning** (alone) | Signals old-school ML, not LLM/agent work |
