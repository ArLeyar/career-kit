# Honesty and Authenticity in Self-Presentation: Perspectives from Traditions and Psychology

> Date: 2026-03-18 | Sources: 18 | Search queries: 9 | Updated: 2026-03-18 (sections 8-10 added)

## TL;DR

- **All traditions are unanimous:** false self-presentation harms the practitioner — not because it's a "sin", but because it destroys inner integrity
- **Psychology confirms:** a fake profile creates self-discrepancy (Higgins) → anxiety + exhaustion from impression management → attracting unsuitable companies
- **P-O fit research:** authentic candidates receive better offers and better fit. Top-10% candidates with high self-verification drive received offers significantly more often
- **Fake facade = reverse filter** — attracts companies that value facade over substance
- **Dzogchen perspective:** deception doesn't "break rules" — it obscures the stream of rigpa. Longchenpa: "trading one's qualities as merchandise among people" = attachment and klesha
- **Strategic honesty ≠ naive openness:** silence about useless truth is acceptable, fabrication is not. The boundary: *how you present a fact* vs *inventing facts*
- **Aurelius Test** — "can you instantly open your mind?" — a practical tool for checking any action for honesty
- **Buddha's Speech Matrix** — 5 cases (truth x usefulness x pleasantness) + 3 tests for "usefulness" (agency, agreement, motivation) + protocol for choosing the right moment for difficult conversations

## 1. Dzogchen / Bon: Deception as an Obstacle to Rigpa

### Milarepa: "My religion is not to deceive myself"

This is perhaps the most direct formulation in the Tibetan tradition. Jetsun Milarepa (1052-1135) posits self-deception as the **only** thing one needs to abstain from. Not lying to others — lying to *oneself*. [Dharma Wheel](https://www.dharmawheel.net/viewtopic.php?t=8563)

### Longchenpa: 30 Stanzas of Advice

Longchenpa (1308-1364) directly addresses several types of false self-presentation:

> **Stanza 2:** "Trading one's qualities as merchandise among people" → leads to attachment and kleshas. Antidote: "tame one's mind"

> **Stanza 5:** Accumulating wealth "through wrong livelihood based on trade, receiving interest, deception and the like" — even virtuous attempts fail if built on a dishonest foundation

> **Stanza 18:** Warning against adopting unusual behavior to appear spiritually advanced — this makes "worldly beings lose faith in what is pure"

> **Stanza 21:** Hypocritically displaying respect to gain material support "will only bind us"

[Lotsawa House — Thirty Pieces of Heart Advice](https://www.lotsawahouse.org/tibetan-masters/longchen-rabjam/30-stanzas-of-advice)

### Kadag + Lhundrup: Ethics from the Nature of Mind

In Dzogchen, ethics is not codified into rules. Two key concepts:

- **Kadag** (ka dag, primordial purity) — recognizing the empty nature of phenomena
- **Lhundrup** (lhun drub, spontaneous presence) — ethical action that spontaneously arises from the nature of mind

They are inseparable. Realization of rigpa doesn't make ethics unimportant — it makes ethics *natural*. Like a jazz musician improvising across multiple genres simultaneously — action from rigpa is spontaneously beneficial. [Vividness — Emptiness, Form, and Dzogchen Ethics](https://vividness.live/emptiness-form-and-dzogchen-ethics)

**Key takeaway for Dzogchen:** deception doesn't "break a rule" — it *obscures* rigpa. Maintaining a facade is an active act of ma-rigpa (ignorance), choosing non-recognition of one's own nature in favor of a constructed image.

### Samaya and Deception

Longchenpa emphasizes: if we don't know what is permitted and not permitted by the vows we hold — that's cause for concern. The true practitioner "remains fully in samaya, but no longer clings to it as an external bond." Deception violates samaya not formally but in essence — it destroys trust in one's own mind. [Rigpa Wiki — Samaya](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Samaya)

## 2. Buddhist Ethics: Right Speech and Upaya

### Samma Vaca (Right Speech) — An Unyielding Position

The Buddha on lying — one of the harshest statements in the entire canon:

> "For one who transgresses in one thing, I tell you, there is no evil deed that is not to be done. What is that one thing? This: deliberate lying."

This is not just one of the rules — it is the **foundation**. A person who lies, "going beyond concern for the world beyond," is capable of any evil. [Access to Insight — Right Speech](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/ptf/dhamma/sacca/sacca4/samma-vaca/index.html)

### Nuances: Intention as Key

The determining factor is the **intention to deceive** (cetana). If a person speaks untruth *believing* it to be true — there is no vow violation. Intention is primary.

The Buddha also distinguished truthful statements by usefulness:
- **Truth + usefulness + pleasantness** → speak, choosing the right time
- **Truth + usefulness + unpleasantness** → speak, carefully choosing the moment
- **Truth + uselessness** → don't speak at all

### Upaya (Skillful Means) — An Exception?

In Mahayana, there is the concept of **upaya kausalya** — skillful means. The parable of the burning house from the Lotus Sutra: a wise father lures children out of a burning house by promising toy carts he doesn't have (intending to give better ones).

**BUT:** this is an exception for a bodhisattva acting from pure compassion for the spiritual benefit of others. The key warning:

> "A true bodhisattva may speak 'non-virtuously' if it serves the spiritual interests of others. But how many true bodhisattvas are there, and can you be *certain* that your motives are pure? In most cases, the answer is no."

A fake profile for one's own employment **does not fall** under upaya — there is no compassionate motivation for the benefit of another being.

### The Bodhisattva and the Vow of Truth

> "In the course of their long training on the path to enlightenment through many lifetimes, a bodhisattva may break all moral precepts, *except the commitment to speak the truth*."

The reason is "very profound" — commitment to truth has significance "beyond ethics and even purification of mind." [Wisdom Library — Right Speech](https://www.wisdomlib.org/buddhism/essay/the-buddhist-path-to-enlightenment-study/d/doc1187806.html)

## 3. Yoga / Hinduism: Satya as Yama

### Patanjali: Yoga Sutras (II.30-36)

Satya (truthfulness) — the second of five yamas (restraints), which Patanjali places *first* in ashtanga yoga — before asanas, pranayama, and meditation:

1. Ahimsa (non-violence)
2. **Satya (truthfulness)**
3. Asteya (non-stealing)
4. Brahmacharya (continence)
5. Aparigraha (non-possessiveness)

### What Satya Means

> "Strict avoidance of all exaggeration, ambiguity, embellishment, pretense, lying, and similar falsifications connected with stating and doing what does not correspond to what we know as truth."

Satya is not just "don't lie out loud." It is **correspondence with reality in thought, speech, and action**.

### Deeper Level: Integrity

> "Another aspect of satya relates to inner truth or integrity — a deeper and more inward practice. Honesty is what we do when others are around and may judge our actions. But to have integrity is to act honestly when no one sees and will never know."

A LinkedIn profile is exactly such a case: no one checks every line. The question isn't "will you get caught," but "who are you when nobody is watching." [Yoga Journal — How to Live by the Yamas](https://www.yogajournal.com/yoga-101/philosophy/beginning-journey/)

### YS II.36: The Power of Truth

Patanjali promises a specific *siddhi* from establishing oneself in satya: when a practitioner is fully established in truthfulness, their words acquire the power of manifestation. What is spoken — comes to pass.

**The reverse is also true:** if speech is contaminated by lies, words lose their power. This directly resonates with the feeling that a fake profile "attracts falsehood."

## 4. Stoicism: Mask vs Essence

### Marcus Aurelius: Radical Transparency

> "Accustom yourself to think only such thoughts as, if someone suddenly asked 'What are you thinking about now?', you could answer with immediate frankness: this and that. Showing that everything in you is straightforward, benevolent, and worthy of a social being."

Marcus Aurelius (121-180) proposes a test: can you instantly, without preparation, reveal the contents of your mind? A fake profile does not pass this test.

### Epictetus: Fidelity as Human Nature

> "People are born for fidelity, and one who damages it damages the distinctive quality of a human being."

Epictetus (50-135) focused on "integrity of will, attitude toward externals, and relationships with others." For Stoics, **the best evidence of a person's philosophy is not what they say but how they behave**. [The Integrity Project — Stoics](https://integrityproject.org/projects/portraits-of-integrity/stoics/)

### Stoic Test for a Fake Profile

Stoics would ask two questions:

1. **Is this under my control?** Yes — the profile can be changed. Therefore, it's a matter of *proairesis* (moral choice)
2. **Does this correspond to my *physis* (nature)?** Trading a false image — a violation of fidelity

> "True peace comes from integrity — doing what is right, not what looks right."

## 5. Existentialism: Mauvaise Foi

### Sartre: The Waiter and Bad Faith

The famous example from "Being and Nothingness" (1943): a waiter in a cafe whose movements are "a little too precise, a little too rapid." He is **playing at** being a waiter — reducing all his existential freedom to a single social function.

> "He is playing at being a waiter in a cafe."

The parallel with a fake profile is direct: you *play at* being a Senior Go Engineer with 8 years of experience, reducing yourself to a constructed image. [Philosophy Break — Sartre's Waiter](https://philosophybreak.com/articles/sartre-waiter-bad-faith-and-the-harms-of-inauthenticity/)

### The Paradox of Bad Faith

> "The one who is lied to and the one who lies are one and the same person, which means: I must know in my capacity as deceiver the truth that is hidden from me in my capacity as the one deceived."

Sartre exposes the **structural impossibility** of self-deception: you simultaneously know the truth (as the author of the fake) and hide it from yourself (as the one who lives with the fake). This creates continuous internal tension.

### En-soi vs Pour-soi

- **En-soi** (being-in-itself) — a thing, a fixed essence. "I am a Senior Go Engineer"
- **Pour-soi** (being-for-itself) — consciousness, freedom, becoming. "I am a person who codes in Go, learns, grows"

A fake profile fixes you as en-soi — turns a living person into a static thing. Authenticity = embracing pour-soi, accepting yourself as *becoming*.

> "We are always becoming and growing, and therefore to regard yourself as some fixed entity is to deceive yourself." — Skye Cleary

### Heidegger: Eigentlichkeit

Heidegger (1889-1976) distinguished:
- **Eigentlichkeit** (ownedness, authenticity) — living from one's own existence
- **Uneigentlichkeit** (un-ownedness) — living as das Man ("the they"), following societal expectations

A fake profile = pure das Man. You write not *for yourself*, but for an abstract recruiter, ATS bot, "the market." This is the definition of Uneigentlichkeit.

## 6. Psychology: Self-Discrepancy and Cognitive Dissonance

### Self-Discrepancy Theory (Higgins, 1987)

E. Tory Higgins identified three selves:

| Self | Description | Fake Profile |
|------|-------------|--------------|
| **Actual self** (real) | Who you actually are | Go developer, ~5 years experience, Southeast Asia |
| **Ideal self** (ideal) | Who you want to become | Senior AI/distributed systems engineer |
| **Ought self** (ought) | Who you "should" be according to others | What's written in the fake profile |

Emotional consequences of discrepancies:
- **Actual ≠ Ideal** → *depressive* emotions (sadness, disappointment)
- **Actual ≠ Ought** → *anxious* emotions (fear, tension, sense of threat)

A fake profile creates a **double hit**: actual ≠ ought (anxiety from mismatch) + actual ≠ ideal (sadness from the gap with who you want to be). [Self-Discrepancy Theory — Psychology Fanatic](https://psychologyfanatic.com/self-discrepancy-theory/)

### Cognitive Dissonance (Festinger, 1957)

Two simultaneous beliefs:
1. "I am an honest person, a Dzogchen practitioner"
2. "My profile contains exaggerations and false data"

This is classic cognitive dissonance. The brain resolves it in one of three ways:
- **Change behavior** → remove the fake (hard — "but what about the market?")
- **Change belief** → "everyone does it, it's normal" (rationalization)
- **Add consonant cognition** → "it's not really a lie, it's *optimization*"

The third option is exactly what Sartre would call mauvaise foi.

### Impostor Syndrome: The Fake Creates a Trap

Paradox: a fake profile *amplifies* impostor syndrome rather than masking it. When you get called for an interview based on fake experience, you enter the room with an internal sense of "I'm not who they think I am." This:
- Increases anxiety during interviews
- Reduces naturalness and confidence
- Creates a loop: fake → anxiety → poor interview → another fake

### Impression Management: The Energy Cost

Maintaining a false image is **active cognitive work**. Research shows that misfit between a person and an organization "creates conditions of exhaustion due to the effort required for self-regulation to *appear* suitable at work." A fake profile starts this exhaustion *before* employment. [Person-Organization Fit Research](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/peps.12581)

## 7. Practical Dimension: The Fake as a Reverse Filter

### Self-Verification Theory: Authenticity = Better P-O Fit

A study by Cable & Kay (Academy of Management Journal) showed:

> "Candidates rated by the organization as being in the top 10% received offers significantly more often if they had a stronger drive toward self-verification [authentic self-presentation]."

Text analysis of interview transcripts revealed: candidates with high self-verification drive were perceived as **less inauthentic and more genuine** by raters. [ResearchGate — The Advantage of Being Oneself](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/320242406_The_advantage_of_being_Oneself_The_role_of_applicant_self-verification_in_organizational_hiring_decisions)

### The "Like Attracts Like" Mechanism

A fake profile works as an **incorrect magnet**:

```
Fake profile                →  Recruiters focused on keywords
  "8 years Go, distributed    →  Companies hiring by formal
   systems, 15k+"                criteria (ATS-driven)
                              →  Companies that value facade
                              →  Misfit → anxiety → burnout

Real profile                →  Recruiters who read substance
  "Go, AI agents, learning    →  Companies that value growth mindset
   fast, honest about gaps"   →  Companies with real projects
                              →  Fit → energy → growth
```

### LinkedIn and Deception Research (Stanford)

Stanford Social Media Lab studied lying in LinkedIn resumes. Key finding: LinkedIn as a platform with public verification (colleagues see the profile) **reduces** gross lying but *increases* minor embellishments — people adjust dates, inflate responsibilities, claim team achievements as their own. [Stanford — The Effect of LinkedIn on Deception in Resumes](https://socialmedialab.sites.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj22976/files/media/file/guillory-cbsn-linkedin.pdf)

### Strategic Honesty vs Naive Openness

No tradition says "be naively open about all your weaknesses." The Buddha distinguished:
- **Truth + usefulness** → speak
- **Truth + uselessness** → stay silent

Applied to a profile:
- ❌ Lying about experience you don't have
- ❌ Writing "I have 3 years experience, I barely know distributed systems"
- ✅ Writing real experience with emphasis on strengths
- ✅ Showing projects that demonstrate competencies
- ✅ Being honest about what you know and showing the ability to learn

## 8. Aurelius Test: Radical Transparency of Mind (Deep Dive)

Marcus Aurelius, "Meditations," Book III:

> "Accustom yourself to think only such thoughts as, if someone suddenly asked 'What are you thinking about now?', you could answer with immediate frankness: this and that. So that from your answer it would be immediately clear that everything in you is straightforward, benevolent, and worthy of a social being."

### How the Test Works

Take any action and apply:

- **Can you instantly explain why this was written/done?**
- **Do you need a pause to prepare an explanation?**
- **If the interested party were sitting next to you right now — what would you feel?**

If you need a pause to construct an explanation — there's something to hide. The keyword is **immediate** frankness.

### Internal Architecture

Aurelius was Emperor of Rome — he had more reasons to lie than nearly anyone in history. He developed this test as a **survival tool**: every lie is a separate "process" in memory. The more such processes — the greater the cognitive load, the fewer resources for real decisions.

Stoics called this **hegemonikon** — the "ruling faculty" of the mind. When the hegemonikon is clear — decisions are clear and fast. When contaminated by contradictions — slowdown, doubt, anxiety.

In Dzogchen, this is rigpa vs ma-rigpa. Aurelius arrived at the same conclusion through logic rather than meditation.

## 9. En-soi vs Pour-soi: Thing vs Freedom (Deep Dive)

Sartre's central distinction from "Being and Nothingness" (1943).

### En-soi (Being-in-itself) — The Mode of a Thing

A rock is a rock. A table is a table. Fixed essence, without freedom, without project, without future. When you fix yourself with a label ("Senior Go Engineer, 8 years") — you turn yourself into such a table.

### Pour-soi (Being-for-itself) — The Mode of Consciousness

A human being is **not a thing**. "Existence precedes essence" (l'existence precede l'essence). Pour-soi:
- Always **does not coincide** with itself (you ≠ your profile, you ≠ your role)
- Is always **directed toward the future** (you are a project, not a result)
- Contains **nothingness** (neant) — the gap between you-now and you-possible

### False Self-Presentation = Flight into En-soi

Freedom is anxiety. Being pour-soi = bearing responsibility every second for who you are becoming. A fake image is an attempt to escape: the certainty of a label instead of the uncertainty of freedom.

Paradox: **you use freedom (pour-soi) to pretend you are not free (en-soi)**. This is the structure of mauvaise foi.

### Authenticity According to Sartre

Authenticite is:
1. **Acknowledge** freedom (I choose every second)
2. **Accept** the anxiety that comes with it
3. **Don't hide** behind roles, labels, "everyone does it"
4. **Take responsibility** for choices without shifting blame to "the market demands it"

## 10. Strategic Honesty: Practical Boundaries

### Four Levels of Self-Presentation

| Level | Example | Verdict |
|-------|---------|---------|
| **1. Fabrication** | Inventing experience you don't have | ❌ Lying in all traditions |
| **2. Embellishment** | "Led team of 10" (actually participated) | ❌ Exaggeration = violates satya |
| **3. Strategic silence** | Not mentioning a gap | ✅ Acceptable |
| **4. Strategic emphasis** | Highlighting strengths first | ✅ This is mastery |

### Buddha's Speech Matrix (complete)

```
         True?      Useful?     Pleasant?     → Action
Case 1:    ✅          ✅          ✅         → Speak (choosing the time)
Case 2:    ✅          ✅          ❌         → Speak (carefully choosing the moment)
Case 3:    ✅          ❌          ✅         → Stay silent
Case 4:    ✅          ❌          ❌         → Stay silent
Case 5:    ❌          —           —          → Stay silent (never speak a lie)
```

**Key point:** Cases 3 and 4 — truth that *doesn't need* to be spoken. The Buddha permits silence about useless truth. But he **never** permits lying (Case 5).

**Silence ≠ lying.** The boundary: between *how you present a fact* and *fabricating a fact*.

### Yama Hierarchy: Ahimsa Constrains Satya

In Patanjali's system, ahimsa (non-violence) stands *above* satya. If truth causes harm — stay silent. But this works one-directionally: ahimsa *constrains* satya (you may stay silent to avoid harm), but **not the reverse** — you cannot lie and call it "non-violence toward yourself."

### How to Determine "Useful" (attha)

The Pali **attha** is not "useful" in a utilitarian sense, but "leads to welfare" for all involved.

**Three tests:**

1. **Agency test** — will the information change the other person's possible actions? Yes → useful. Expands their space of choice → useful. Narrows it (humiliates, hurts without purpose) → not useful.

2. **Agreement test** — is there an explicit or implicit commitment that this information affects? There's an agreement → useful (the other person builds decisions on trust). No agreement → evaluate using tests 1 and 3.

3. **Motivation test** — what does the statement serve? Restoring another's freedom of choice → attha. Offloading your own guilt at someone else's expense → not attha.

**Litmus test:** if you are spending energy justifying *why not to speak* — that's a signal. Truly useless truth doesn't require justifying silence.

### How to Choose the Moment (kalena) — For Case 2

The Buddha used **kalena** — "when ripe." Not "someday" and not "the perfect moment" (it doesn't exist). Four conditions for a *good enough* moment:

1. **Both parties are resourced** — rested, fed, not rushing. Not after a fight, not at 2 AM.

2. **You are ready to listen, not just speak** — if you are internally rehearsing *your* speech but not imagining the other's reaction — you are not ready.

3. **No hidden deadline** — not before departure, not before leaving for work. There is time ahead to be with the consequences.

4. **Intention is pure right now** — motivation changes hour by hour. Check at the moment you are about to open your mouth.

**Anti-pattern:** waiting for the perfect moment = the most elegant form of avoidance. A good enough moment is when all four conditions above = ✅.

**Protocol:**
1. Start with framing: "I need to tell you something. It's unpleasant, but I'm choosing honesty."
2. Fact, not story: brief, without preamble.
3. Pause — give space to respond.
4. Listen. Don't get defensive. "Why" — later, if asked.

## Recommendations

1. **Switch to a real profile** — this is not weakness but a strategic advantage. Research confirms: authenticity = better P-O fit = better offers
2. **Strategic truth, not naive openness** — emphasis on real projects (your-project, your-tool, CompanyA), demonstrating growth trajectory. Don't embellish, but don't be self-deprecating either
3. **Marcus Aurelius Test** — before writing a line in your profile, ask: "can I say this with immediate frankness?"
4. **Your-project as proof-of-work** — an open source Rust project speaks louder than any embellished resume. Code doesn't lie
5. **Consider the fake profile as a learning experience** (3/5 profile!) — don't regret it, integrate it. You tried, saw the result, corrected course. This is literally how 3/5 works
